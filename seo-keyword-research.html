Keyword Research

How to find the terms worth targeting, and rank them by what they'll actually return.

On this page

  1. Start with intent, not volume
  2. Where to source real queries
  3. Scoring and prioritizing keywords
  4. Mapping keywords to pages

Start with intent, not volume

Every query falls roughly into one of four intents: informational (learning something), navigational (looking for a specific site), commercial (comparing options), or transactional (ready to act). A page built for the wrong intent rarely ranks well no matter how well it's written, because it isn't answering the question the searcher actually had.

Before touching a keyword tool, sort your target terms by intent and match each one to the page type that satisfies it — a guide for informational terms, a comparison or landing page for commercial and transactional ones.

Where to source real queries

Search Console is the most underused source most sites have: it shows the exact queries already bringing traffic, including long-tail phrasing no keyword tool would surface. Autocomplete and "people also ask" boxes reveal how real users phrase a question, not how a tool categorizes it.

Competitor gaps are worth a deliberate look too — pages ranking above you that you don't have an equivalent for are usually the highest-leverage opportunities, because the demand is already proven.

Scoring and prioritizing keywords

Volume alone is a poor prioritization signal. Weigh it against difficulty (can a new or mid-authority page realistically compete), intent match (does this term convert into what your business actually offers), and cannibalization risk (do you already have a page that could serve this query).

Mapping keywords to pages

Assign one primary keyword per page and cluster closely related terms underneath it rather than creating a separate page for every variation — search engines already understand that "running shoes for flat feet" and "best running shoes flat feet" are the same query, and splitting them just dilutes both pages.

Keep it living

Keyword research isn't a one-time spreadsheet. Revisit priorities every quarter as your site earns authority — terms that were out of reach at launch often become realistic six months in.

Action checklist

  • ✓Cluster keywords by intent before writing anything
  • ✓Check existing rankings before creating a new page, to avoid cannibalization
  • ✓Cross-check volume against a second data source before trusting it
  • ✓Note which pages currently rank in the top 5 for each priority term
  • ✓Set a realistic difficulty ceiling for a newer domain
