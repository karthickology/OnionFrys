Keyword Research

How to find the terms worth targeting, and rank them by what they'll actually return.

On this page

  1. Start with intent, not volume
  2. Where to source real queries
  3. Scoring and prioritizing keywords
  4. Mapping keywords to pages

Start with intent, not volume

"Every query falls roughly into one of four search intents: informational intent (learning something), navigational intent (looking for a specific site), commercial investigation (comparing options, reading reviews, or researching brands), or transactional intent (ready to act, buy, or sign up). A page built for the wrong user intent rarely ranks well—no matter how well it's written—because it fails to deliver query satisfaction or match the searcher's journey at that specific stage.

Before you even touch a keyword research tool, sort your target terms by keyword categorization and intent mapping. Then, align each one with the content format that satisfies it—a how-to guide, ultimate guide, or educational blog post for informational terms; a versus comparison, product roundup, or feature breakdown for commercial investigation; and a dedicated landing page, product page, or pricing page with clear calls-to-action (CTAs) for commercial and transactional ones. This approach—often called intent-based SEO or audience-first content strategy—ensures your on-page SEO, content relevance, and conversion optimization all work together to boost organic rankings and click-through rates (CTR).".

Where to source real queries

"Google Search Console is the most underused source for organic keyword discovery that most sites have: it shows the exact search queries already driving impressions and clicks, including long-tail keywords and zero-volume keywords that no third-party keyword tool would ever surface. Google Autocomplete and the "People Also Ask" (PAA) boxes reveal how real users phrase a question-based query, offering invaluable natural language data that no tool's search volume metrics can replicate—this is pure user-generated keyword intelligence.

Competitor keyword gaps are worth a deliberate look too. Rank gap analysis—identifying pages ranking above you that you don't have an equivalent content asset for—are usually the highest-leverage keyword opportunities, because the search demand is already proven. This process, often called competitive SEO analysis or missing content auditing, helps you build a priority keyword list based on proven traffic potential rather than guesswork. Combining these sources gives you a holistic keyword strategy that fuels both content ideation and technical SEO."

Scoring and prioritizing keywords

"Search volume alone is a poor prioritization signal—it's a vanity metric that misleads more than it helps. Instead, weigh it against keyword difficulty (KD)—can a new or mid-authority page realistically compete against domain authority (DA) giants? Factor in intent alignment—does this term pass the commercial intent test and actually convert into what your value proposition and product-market fit actually offer? And don't forget keyword cannibalization risk—do you already have a competing page, existing content asset, or ranking URL that could serve this query with some content consolidation or merge and redirect strategy? This holistic approach—often called search term prioritization, opportunity scoring, or value-based keyword selection—ensures you're not wasting crawl budget or editorial resources on high-volume, low-conversion keywords that generate vanity traffic without business impact. True SEO efficiency comes from scoring each term against a weighted scoring model that combines attainability, relevance, and ROI potential—not just how many people type it into Google."

Mapping keywords to pages

"Assign one primary keyword per page and cluster closely related terms underneath it as secondary keywords and semantic variations—rather than creating a separate page for every keyword variation. Search engines already understand that "running shoes for flat feet" and "best running shoes flat feet" are the same search query, and splitting them just dilutes authority and cannibalizes rankings for both pages.

This approach—called keyword clustering, topic clustering, or content siloing—builds a single authoritative pillar page supported by cluster content. It signals topical authority, improves internal linking, eliminates duplicate content, and gives users one definitive resource rather than fragmented pages. Focus on page-level optimization with one clear target keyword and a supporting keyword list for better ranking consolidation and stronger domain authority.".

Keep it living

Keyword research isn't a one-time spreadsheet. Revisit priorities every quarter as your site earns authority — terms that were out of reach at launch often become realistic six months in.

Action checklist

  • ✓Cluster keywords by intent before writing anything
  • ✓Check existing rankings before creating a new page, to avoid cannibalization
  • ✓Cross-check volume against a second data source before trusting it
  • ✓Note which pages currently rank in the top 5 for each priority term
  • ✓Set a realistic difficulty ceiling for a newer domain
