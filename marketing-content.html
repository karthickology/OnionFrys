Content Marketing

Publishing built to compound: useful the day it ships, and still useful a year later.

On this page

  1. Pick a content model, not just topics

Pick a content model, not just topics

"A pillar-and-cluster model means a few comprehensive pillar pages, each supported by focused cluster pages that dive into specific subtopics. This gives every piece of content a clear place to link to and from—making your site more organized and easier to navigate.

It's the same structure this guide uses: five master topics, each broken into specific questions underneath. A strong topic cluster strategy helps both visitors and search engines understand your site's structure and expertise."

Writing for a specific reader

"Content written for 'everyone interested in marketing' usually ends up saying nothing useful to anyone in particular. It's filled with vague advice that sounds safe but offers no real help. When you try to speak to everyone, you connect with no one—readers bounce quickly, feeling like they've wasted their time.

The pieces that actually get shared and cited are written for one specific reader in one specific situation—like a SaaS founder struggling with churn or an e-commerce owner fighting cart abandonment. They use concrete examples and real numbers instead of generic tips like 'post consistently.' That specificity makes content valuable, memorable, and worth linking to."

Distribution is half the job

"Publishing is the easy half. The real leverage comes after—when you turn one piece of long-form content into multiple formats. That same guide can become social posts, an email newsletter, a short video script, and even a slide deck—each reaching people who would never have found the original.

This content repurposing strategy stretches your effort further without starting from scratch every time. One idea, many formats, more eyes on your work."

Measuring what content actually does

"Pageviews are the easiest metric to look at—and one of the least useful on their own. A high pageview count can look impressive, but it doesn't tell you if anyone actually read, understood, or acted on your content. It's a vanity metric that can easily mislead.

Instead, focus on metrics that show real engagement: engaged time (how long people actually stick around), scroll depth (how far they bother to scroll), and assisted conversions (did this page help drive a sale that happened elsewhere?). These tell a much more honest story. We cover how to set these up in GA4 in the Analytics section."

Write the one thing, then reuse it

The highest-leverage move is rarely publishing more — it's getting more distribution out of the handful of pieces that are genuinely worth someone's time.

Action checklist

  • ✓Define one specific reader before drafting, not a general audience
  • ✓Plan at least two repurposed formats before a piece publishes
  • ✓Link every new piece to and from a relevant pillar page
  • ✓Track engaged time and assisted conversions, not just pageviews
  • ✓Retire or refresh pieces that have gone stale rather than leaving them up as-is
