Content Marketing

Publishing built to compound: useful the day it ships, and still useful a year later.

On this page

  1. Pick a content model, not just topics
  2. Writing for a specific reader
  3. Distribution is half the job
  4. Measuring what content actually does

Pick a content model, not just topics

A pillar-and-cluster model — a few comprehensive master pages, each supported by focused sub-pages — gives every piece of content somewhere to link to and from. It's the same structure this site itself uses: five master topics, each broken into the specific questions underneath it.

Writing for a specific reader

Content aimed at "everyone interested in marketing" tends to say nothing useful to anyone in particular. The pieces that get shared and cited are usually written for one specific reader in one specific situation, with concrete examples instead of generic advice.

Distribution is half the job

Publishing is the easy half. A single piece of long-form content can be repurposed into social posts, an email, a short video script, and a slide deck — reaching people who would never have found the original.

Measuring what content actually does

Pageviews are the easiest metric to look at and one of the least useful on their own. Engaged time, scroll depth, and assisted conversions (did this page contribute to a sale that closed elsewhere) tell a more honest story — see the Analytics section for how to set those up in GA4.

Write the one thing, then reuse it

The highest-leverage move is rarely publishing more — it's getting more distribution out of the handful of pieces that are genuinely worth someone's time.

Action checklist

  • ✓Define one specific reader before drafting, not a general audience
  • ✓Plan at least two repurposed formats before a piece publishes
  • ✓Link every new piece to and from a relevant pillar page
  • ✓Track engaged time and assisted conversions, not just pageviews
  • ✓Retire or refresh pieces that have gone stale rather than leaving them up as-is
