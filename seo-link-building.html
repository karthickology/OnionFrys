Link Building

Earning citations that hold up over time, not renting them.

On this page

  1. What actually earns links
  2. Manual outreach that doesn't read like spam
  3. Digital PR and relationship-based links
  4. What to avoid

What actually earns links

"Links come naturally to things worth linking to—like original data, a genuinely useful free tool, or unique research nobody else has done. Create something truly valuable, and people will link to it without you asking. That's how earned links work.

Content built just to get links—without being truly useful—tends to attract low-quality links from the same kind of sites. And those don't help as much as they look like they do. Focus on creating link-worthy content that actually helps people, and the right links will follow."

Manual outreach that doesn't read like spam

"Good link outreach is specific. It mentions the exact page you're pitching, explains why your content fits naturally, and offers value before asking for anything. Personalize each message—show you've actually looked at their work.

Mass template emails are easy to spot and easy to delete. Generic pitches waste everyone's time. A thoughtful, tailored email is more work, but it's also the one that actually gets responses and earns real links."

Digital PR and relationship-based links

"Journalists, podcast hosts, and newsletter writers are always looking for a good data point, a strong quote, or an interesting expert angle. They need helpful voices to feature—and you can be that voice.

Instead of sending random one-off pitches, focus on building real relationships with a few key people in your niche. Stay in touch, be helpful, and share useful insights over time. A small set of ongoing connections will get you more coverage and mentions than a hundred cold emails ever will."

What to avoid

"Paid links, link exchanges, and private blog networks (PBNs) break search engine rules and can get you a manual penalty. Even if they seem to work for a while, they're a risk sitting on your site, waiting to be caught.

Avoid these shortcuts—they're not worth the damage. Natural links from real sites take more work, but they're safe, lasting, and actually build long-term authority."

Earned beats rented

A link you earned because something was genuinely useful keeps working even after algorithms change. A link you paid for is a liability the moment it's discovered.

Action checklist

  • ✓Build at least one genuinely link-worthy asset before starting outreach
  • ✓Personalize outreach to the specific page you're pitching
  • ✓Track which outreach angles actually get replies, not just links
  • ✓Audit your backlink profile periodically for spammy or toxic links
  • ✓Never trade money or product for a followed link
