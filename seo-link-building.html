Link Building

Earning citations that hold up over time, not renting them.

On this page

  1. What actually earns links
  2. Manual outreach that doesn't read like spam
  3. Digital PR and relationship-based links
  4. What to avoid

What actually earns links

Links accumulate naturally around things worth citing: original data, a genuinely useful free tool, or research nobody else has done. Content built purely to attract links (without being independently useful) tends to attract low-quality links from the same tier of site, which helps less than it looks like it should.

Manual outreach that doesn't read like spam

Outreach that works is specific: it references the actual page you're pitching a link to, explains why your resource fits into it, and gives something of value before asking for anything. Mass-personalized templates are usually easy to spot and easy to ignore.

Digital PR and relationship-based links

Journalists, podcast hosts, and newsletter writers are constantly looking for a data point, a quote, or an expert angle. Building a small set of ongoing relationships in your niche tends to outperform one-off outreach blasts over a year or two.

What to avoid

Paid links, link exchanges, and private blog networks violate search engine guidelines and carry real risk of a manual penalty. Even when they work short-term, they're a liability sitting on your site waiting to be discovered.

Earned beats rented

A link you earned because something was genuinely useful keeps working even after algorithms change. A link you paid for is a liability the moment it's discovered.

Action checklist

  • ✓Build at least one genuinely link-worthy asset before starting outreach
  • ✓Personalize outreach to the specific page you're pitching
  • ✓Track which outreach angles actually get replies, not just links
  • ✓Audit your backlink profile periodically for spammy or toxic links
  • ✓Never trade money or product for a followed link
