Analytics & GA4

Custom Reports

A report is only useful if it answers a question someone's actually asking.

On this page

  1. Start from the decision, not the dashboard
  2. Explorations vs. standard reports
  3. Building a report people will open
  4. Automating the boring parts

Start from the decision, not the dashboard

Before building anything, list the three or four questions stakeholders actually ask on a recurring basis — which channel is driving the most qualified traffic, is this campaign paying for itself, is content engagement trending up or down. Build reports around those, not around every metric GA4 happens to offer.

Explorations vs. standard reports

Standard reports are built for recurring, stable questions. Explorations are better suited to a one-off, open-ended question — why did conversions dip last Tuesday, what path do converting users actually take — and shouldn't be rebuilt as a permanent dashboard unless the question itself recurs.

Building a report people will open

A report with twelve charts gets opened once and ignored. One clear chart, a comparison period, and a one-line takeaway gets opened every week. Clarity is a design decision, not an afterthought.

Automating the boring parts

Scheduled exports and a Looker Studio connection remove the manual work of pulling the same numbers every week, freeing time for the part that actually needs a person: interpreting what changed and why.

A dashboard nobody opens is worse than no dashboard

It creates the appearance of measurement without any of the benefit. Fewer, sharper reports beat comprehensive ones nobody has time to read.

Action checklist

  • ✓List the recurring questions a report needs to answer before building it
  • ✓Use Explorations for one-off questions instead of building a permanent report
  • ✓Limit recurring reports to the charts that actually get acted on
  • ✓Add a comparison period to every trend chart
  • ✓Automate recurring exports so reporting time goes to interpretation
