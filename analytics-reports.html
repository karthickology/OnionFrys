Analytics & GA4

Custom Reports

A report is only useful if it answers a question someone's actually asking.

On this page

  1. Start from the decision, not the dashboard
  2. Explorations vs. standard reports
  3. Building a report people will open
  4. Automating the boring parts

Start from the decision, not the dashboard

"Before building anything, list the three or four questions stakeholders actually ask on a recurring basis—like which channel is driving the most qualified traffic?, is this campaign paying for itself?, or is content engagement trending up or down? These are the questions that drive real decisions. Start there.

Build your reports around those questions, not around every metric GA4 happens to offer. A dashboard packed with every number available looks thorough, but it buries what actually matters. Focus on answering the questions that get asked week after week—and ignore the rest."

Explorations vs. standard reports

"Standard reports are built for recurring, stable questions—the ones you need to check every week or month without rethinking the setup. They're efficient, consistent, and reliable for ongoing monitoring. Explorations are better suited to a one-off, open-ended question—like why did conversions dip last Tuesday? or what path do converting users actually take? They let you dig deep and follow the data wherever it leads.

But Explorations shouldn't be rebuilt as a permanent dashboard unless the question itself becomes a regular one. If you're asking it every week, build it into a standard report. If it's a one-time curiosity, explore it, learn from it, and move on."

Building a report people will open

"A report with twelve charts gets opened once and ignored. It's overwhelming, unfocused, and asks too much of the person reading it. They close it, move on, and never come back—because finding the insight feels like work.

One clear chart, a comparison period, and a one-line takeaway gets opened every week. It tells a story instantly, without effort. Clarity is a design decision, not an afterthought—build for understanding, not for completeness."

Automating the boring parts

"Scheduled exports and a Looker Studio connection remove the manual work of pulling the same numbers every week. No more downloading CSV files, rebuilding the same tables, or wasting Monday mornings on repetitive tasks. The data just shows up when you need it.

That frees time for the part that actually needs a person: interpreting what changed and why. The machine handles the pulling; you handle the thinking. Automation buys you back hours to focus on insights, not spreadsheets."

A dashboard nobody opens is worse than no dashboard

It creates the appearance of measurement without any of the benefit. Fewer, sharper reports beat comprehensive ones nobody has time to read.

Action checklist

  • ✓List the recurring questions a report needs to answer before building it
  • ✓Use Explorations for one-off questions instead of building a permanent report
  • ✓Limit recurring reports to the charts that actually get acted on
  • ✓Add a comparison period to every trend chart
  • ✓Automate recurring exports so reporting time goes to interpretation
