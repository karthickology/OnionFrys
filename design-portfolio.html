Portfolio Showcase

A portfolio's job is to make your thinking visible, not just your Figma files.

On this page

  1. Choosing which projects to include
  2. Structuring a project page
  3. Showing process, not just polish
  4. Presentation details that signal craft

Choosing which projects to include

"Three or four projects covered in real depth demonstrate more than ten covered in a paragraph each. A shallow portfolio reads as a list of things you touched—it shows you were there, but not what you actually did or why it mattered.

A deep one reads as evidence of how you think. It shows your process, your decisions, the tradeoffs you made, and the results you delivered. Depth over breadth tells a story. Shallow breadth just fills space."

Structuring a project page

"A consistent structure—problem, constraints, process, outcome—lets a reviewer compare projects quickly and signals that the thinking, not just the visuals, is the point of the page. It makes each case easy to scan and understand without guessing what you're trying to say.

When every project follows the same format, the reviewer focuses on what actually matters: how you approached the work, what tradeoffs you made, and what resulted from your decisions. The structure itself becomes proof that your work is deliberate, not accidental."

Showing process, not just polish

"Early sketches, rejected directions, and an explanation of what changed between them and the final version tell a reviewer more about your judgment than the finished screen alone ever could. Anyone can polish a final output—showing the messy middle reveals how you think under uncertainty.

It demonstrates that you evaluate options, not just execute the first idea. You can explain why you moved away from something and toward something else. That kind of reflection is what separates a designer from someone who just made the thing look pretty."

Presentation details that signal craft

"Consistent framing across project screenshots, real device contexts instead of generic mockup templates, and a portfolio site that itself loads quickly—these all quietly signal the same care a reviewer hopes to see in your actual design work. The presentation reflects your standards just as much as the work itself.

If your portfolio feels rushed, slow, or visually inconsistent, it creates doubt. If everything is polished, thoughtful, and fast, it builds confidence before a single project is even read. The medium is the message."

Depth is the differentiator

In a portfolio, three projects explained well will out-perform ten projects explained thinly, almost every time.

Action checklist

  • ✓Narrow the portfolio to projects you can explain in real depth
  • ✓Use a consistent problem → constraints → process → outcome structure
  • ✓Include at least one early sketch or rejected direction per project
  • ✓Keep screenshot framing and device context consistent across projects
  • ✓Check that the portfolio site itself loads quickly on mobile
