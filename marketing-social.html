Social Media

Showing up consistently on the platforms your audience already uses — without a whole new strategy for each one.

On this page

  1. Choosing platforms deliberately
  2. A format-first content system
  3. Community over broadcast

Choosing platforms deliberately

"Being present everywhere usually means being mediocre everywhere. Spreading yourself across every platform dilutes your effort, and your content ends up feeling thin and unfocused. It's better to do one thing well than ten things poorly.

Pick the one or two platforms where your actual audience spends time and a format they actually respond to—whether that's long-form LinkedIn posts, short TikTok videos, or weekly newsletters. Treat the rest as optional rather than obligatory. Focus wins over frantic."

A format-first content system

"Rather than writing native content from scratch for every platform, adapt from a single core piece. A long-form article becomes a short-form video script, a Twitter thread, or a carousel post. The underlying idea stays the same—only the format changes.

This content adaptation approach saves time, keeps your messaging consistent, and ensures you're not reinventing the wheel for every channel. One strong idea, repackaged for each platform, reaches more people with less effort."

Community over broadcast

"Replies, comments, and DMs compound in a way that broadcast posting doesn't. Public posts get seen, but real conversations build connection. Engaging in the comments, answering DMs thoughtfully, and showing up in other people's discussions creates trust that one-way content never can.

The accounts that build real loyalty spend as much time engaging with others as they do posting their own content. It's not just about being heard—it's about being present in the conversation."

What to track

"Reach and impressions describe exposure—not impact. They tell you how many people scrolled past your post, not whether anyone actually cared. Big numbers look nice, but they're shallow without real action behind them.

Engagement rate and—where you can track it—referral traffic to your own site are better signals of whether your social activity is actually building something. Clicks, replies, and visits to your content show real interest. Attention is vanity; action is value."

Depth over presence

One platform done consistently and well outperforms five platforms done inconsistently, almost every time.

Action checklist

  • ✓Confirm your audience is actually active on a platform before committing to it
  • ✓Build a repurposing workflow from core content instead of writing from scratch per platform
  • ✓Budget time for replies and community engagement, not just posting
  • ✓Track referral traffic and engagement rate over raw reach
  • ✓Review platform choice quarterly rather than assuming it's still right
