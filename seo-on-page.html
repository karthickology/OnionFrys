On-Page SEO

Structuring a page so intent, hierarchy, and crawlability are obvious at a glance.

Title tags and meta descriptions that earn the click

"Title tags have one job beyond ranking: getting clicked in a search engine results page (SERP) full of competitors. That means a unique title tag with a specific, compelling angle for every page—never a template title repeated with one word swapped out—with the primary keyword appearing naturally near the front-load position. This is title tag optimization at its finest, improving both click-through rate (CTR) and relevance signals.

Meta descriptions don't directly affect ranking factors, but they're the closest thing to ad copy you get in organic search results. Write them like a value proposition and a reason to click, not a keyword-stuffed summary that reads like a robot wrote it. A well-crafted meta description acts as a compelling snippet that boosts SERP visibility, drives qualified traffic, and bridges the gap between search intent and user action. Think of it as your free ad space—use it to promise solutions, not just repeat target keywords.

Together, these elements form the foundation of SERP optimization—ensuring your pages don't just rank, but earn clicks that turn into visitors, leads, and customers."

Heading hierarchy and content structure

"One main heading (H1) per page, and subheadings that flow naturally underneath it—H3s belong under the H2 they're connected to, never skipping a level. This isn't just for search engine bots—it's how most people read. A clean heading structure lets skimmers grasp your page's flow in seconds, find what they need fast, and stick around longer.

Good content structure helps both Google and your visitors. It makes your page easier to read, easier to navigate, and shows search engines what your content is really about. Think of your H1 as the big idea, your H2s as main sections, and your H3s as details under each section. Skip a level, and you confuse everyone. Get it right, and you've built a clear path that works for both people and algorithms."

Internal linking with intent

"Anchor text should describe the page you're linking to—not just say "click here" or "learn more." Use descriptive anchor text that tells readers and search engines exactly what to expect. Link to your priority pages from wherever it's genuinely relevant—internal links are one of the few ranking signals entirely within your control, and they're how link equity (or authority) flows from your strongest pages to newer or deeper ones.

Think of internal linking as building a roadmap for both users and search engines. Good link structure helps visitors discover more of your content, keeps them on your site longer, and tells Google which pages matter most. Link naturally, use relevant anchor phrases, and connect related content—it's one of the smartest, most controllable SEO wins you can make."

Media and accessibility

"Every image needs alt text that describes what it shows. This helps accessibility—so screen readers can describe images to visually impaired users—and it helps search engines, because they can't see images, only read the text about them. Write descriptive alt text that's clear and helpful, not keyword-stuffed.

Image file size also matters. Heavy images slow down your page, and speed is a ranking factor through Core Web Vitals. Compress your images, use the right format, and keep things fast—it keeps visitors happy and Google happy too."

Compounding, not competing

On-page work and technical SEO reinforce each other. A perfectly written page still won't rank if a crawler can't reach it — check the Technical SEO cluster alongside this one.

Action checklist

  • ✓Write a unique title and meta description for every page
  • ✓Confirm exactly one H1 exists per page
  • ✓Check heading levels don't skip (H2 → H4 with no H3)
  • ✓Add descriptive alt text to every meaningful image
  • ✓Link to at least two other relevant pages from any new page
